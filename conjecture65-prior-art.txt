Prior-art search for the perpendicular perturbation conjecture
19 November 2026, New York time

Target: Fish, King, Miller, Palsson and Wahlenmayer, Integers 20 (2020)
A96, Conjecture 6.5. Definitions 2.8, 2.10, 4.1 and 4.4 control the
statement. The exact quantifier is one fixed non-strictly convex norm
and all sufficiently large cardinalities.
https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/u96/u96.pdf

Closest constructions within the source:
* Theorem 1.2 uses a straight boundary segment. The constructions are
  perpendicular perturbations, as the authors note in Example 4.2.
* Theorem 1.3 gives the distance-equality pattern on a small Euclidean
  circular arc. It does not exclude four points sharing a norm circle.
* Example 4.3 has collinear triples for n >= 5 (see Example 4.5).
* Theorem 1.5 proves the conjectured classification for the maximum norm.
  Transporting the classification by a linear isometry does not give
  a counterexample to the parallel-displacements formulation.
* Proposition 21 of Martini, Swanepoel and Weiss proves the intersection
  property of planar positive homothets used in the present proof.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.2900

Additional bibliographic cross-check, 19 November 2026:
* Corollary 3.4, page 16 of the original article, also gives the
  no-three-collinear condition for circular arcs. It stops there. An
  entirely Euclidean norm satisfies its hypotheses and has all the
  chosen points on a norm circle, so this corollary alone cannot
  supply the required no-four-on-a-norm-circle property.
* zbMATH lists the published article as Zbl 1465.52027, record
  7306642, and links DOI 10.5281/zenodo.10802297. This deposit has
  a separate OpenAlex record, W6967587290. Both that record and
  W2973400484 returned zero indexed citations. DataCite reported zero
  citations to the deposit, and OpenCitations returned an empty list
  for that DOI. These are coverage observations, not priority proofs.
* GitHub issue/PR searches using the deposit DOI and the MathDB
  problem number together with "crescent" returned no matches.
* Semantic Scholar API returned HTTP 429. Crossref bibliographic
  search did not identify this article among the first two results;
  neither was counted as a negative citation check.
